# Testing Kilalao with children

**Purpose:** find out where children understand the activities and where they struggle or lose interest. The method is to watch real children play, without explaining. This guide is for a parent, teacher or volunteer running a short session.

**Bring back:** the anonymous report from the app (adult corner → *Testing with children* → *Build report*) and, if you like, a few sentences of your own.

## Before the session

- **Consent:**
  - Ask a parent or guardian for permission.
  - Tell the child they are helping to *test the app*, not being tested themselves.
  - Any child can stop at any time, for any reason.
- **Privacy:**
  - Don't take photos or videos unless the parent agrees in writing.
  - The app report contains no names: children appear as "Child 1, age 5".
- **Device:**
  - Charge the device and turn the sound on, at a medium volume.
  - Install the latest APK, or open the website once while online so it works offline.
- **Profile:** in the adult corner (hold the top-right lock for 2 seconds, then answer the sum), go to *Learning*, then:
  - add the child with their **real age** and **language**;
  - use a first name or a nickname; it never leaves the device.
- **Voices:** for French, check that the device has a French voice (Android: Settings → System → Languages → Text-to-speech). Malagasy narration needs recorded clips (see `app/audio/README.md`).
- **Place:** a quiet table. Sit beside the child, slightly behind, so the app stays the focus.

## The session (15–20 minutes)

| Time | What happens | Your role |
|---|---|---|
| 2 min | Show the shelf. Say: *"This is a playroom. The book with the path is for learning with the owl."* | Point once, then let the child choose. |
| 10–12 min | The child plays **Learn**: chooses a friend, does a quest, goes back to the village and chooses again. | **Watch, don't teach.** Only help if the child asks twice or is clearly upset. If you help, tap *Needed an adult to explain*. |
| 3 min | Free play with any toy on the shelf. | Relax; this is the reward. |
| 2 min | Ask the questions below. | Write the answers in the note box. |

### What to watch for (tap the matching button as it happens)

| Button | Signs |
|---|---|
| *Understood what to do* | Starts the task without looking at you; imitates the hand from the demonstration. |
| *Hesitated / unsure* | Hand hovers, looks at you, taps at random. |
| *Needed an adult to explain* | You had to say or show something. |
| *Enjoyed it* | Smiles, talks to the characters, wants another quest. |
| *Looked bored* | Looks away, taps fast without looking, sighs. |
| *Got frustrated* | Complains, taps hard, pushes the device away. |
| *Liked choosing friends* | Talks about which friend to help, or about the village. |
| *Voice hard to understand* | The child doesn't react to the spoken question, or you can't understand the sound. Important for letter sounds. |
| *Taps missed / hard to tap* | Taps land but nothing happens, or the wrong thing moves. |
| *Wanted to stop* | Asks to stop or to do something else. Let them. |

Each tap is saved with the quest the child is on, so the report shows *where* it happened.

### Questions to ask at the end (age 4 and up)

Show three faces 🙂 😐 🙁 drawn on paper and let the child point.

1. *How did you like helping the friends?*
2. *Was anything too hard? Show me which one.* Open the village and let the child point.
3. *Did the owl's hand help you?*
4. *Would you like to play again tomorrow?*

## After the session

1. In the adult corner, open *Testing with children*, choose the child, then tap **Build report** and **Copy report**.
2. Paste the report into an email or message, and add anything you noticed that the buttons don't cover.
3. Send it to the person collecting results.
4. Optionally tap **Clear observations for this child**. Learning progress is kept, so the child can carry on.

## Reading the report

For each child and each skill, the report shows:
- **started/left/stuck:** quests started, quests left before the end, and quests ended early because the child was stuck twice;
- **right first time:** answers to independent items that were right on the first try;
- **average hint level:** 0 means no help; 1 means look again; 2 means the hand did the first step; 3 means a full demonstration;
- **median time per item.**

What to look for:
- **Many "left" on one skill:** the activity may be unclear or boring. Look at the observation tags around it.
- **Hint level often 3 at the right age:** the demonstration or the sequence needs work, or the skill is placed too early.
- **Very fast and always right:** placement is too low. Check the child's age setting.
- **"Voice hard to understand" on reading skills:** recorded sounds are needed for that language.

## How many children

Five children per age band (3–4, 5–6, 7–8) finds most problems that matter. Mix confident and hesitant children, and both languages if possible. Repeat after each round of changes.
