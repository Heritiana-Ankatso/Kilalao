# Benchmark: what the best educational apps do, and what Kilalao takes from them

The owner asked for Kilalao to become "the best educational gaming app". This page records what we studied, why those apps work (where there is evidence), and what Kilalao does as a result. It also says plainly where the evidence is weak.

## The yardstick: the four pillars

**Hirsh-Pasek, Zosh, Golinkoff, Gray, Robb and Kaufman (2015), "Putting Education in 'Educational' Apps: Lessons from the Science of Learning"**, *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 16(1).

Children learn from an app when it supports four pillars, inside a scaffolded learning goal:

| Pillar | Meaning | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| **Active** | Minds-on thinking, not just tapping | Swiping through, tapping to make things happen |
| **Engaged** | Attention stays on the learning | "Enhancements" such as hotspots, sounds and rewards that pull attention away |
| **Meaningful** | Connected to the child's life and what they already know | Isolated drills with no link to the real world |
| **Socially interactive** | Talking about it with other people, on or off the screen | The child plays alone and no adult ever hears about it |

The authors note that distracting enhancements (hotspots, sound effects, animations unrelated to the content) **hurt** learning.

A follow-up content analysis (**Meyer et al., 2021**, *Journal of Children and Media*; open access at [PMC8916741](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8916741/)) scored the most downloaded "educational" apps against these pillars. Most scored low, especially on engagement, and free apps were the most distracting (ads, pop-ups, reward screens).

Kilalao uses the four pillars as its checklist. The table at the end shows how each one is met.

## The apps

### Khan Academy Kids

- **What it is:** a broad, free curriculum for ages 2–8 across reading, maths, logic and social-emotional learning. It has an adaptive learning path, a book library, a cast of characters, and no ads or purchases.
- **Why it works:** breadth in one calm place, a path that adapts so the child is not lost, and stories alongside drills. Reviewers regularly rank it the best free all-round app.
- **What Kilalao takes:** several subjects in one app (numbers, reading, logic and coding, feelings), an adaptive path per child, a story library, recurring characters, and no ads or purchases.

### Duolingo ABC

- **What it is:** a reading app with short lessons and read-along stories.
- **Why it works:** lessons are short enough to finish, and they feel like a game.
- **What Kilalao takes:** short quests (one skill each), and read-along books in which each word lights up as it is read and can be touched to hear it again.
- **What Kilalao does not take:** streaks and reward animations. Kilalao keeps rewards intrinsic (a friend helped, the village growing); see [PEDAGOGY.md](PEDAGOGY.md).

### Teach Your Monster to Read

- **What it is:** a narrative journey in which the child creates their own creature and learns systematic phonics along the way.
- **Why it works:** systematic phonics is well supported, and the story gives the practice a purpose.
- **Evidence:** we found no independent trial of the app itself. The closest evidence is the **GraphoGame Rime** randomised trial (**Ahmed et al., 2020**, *Frontiers in Education*), which tested a similar game-based phonics approach.
- **What Kilalao takes:** systematic phonics in English and the syllabic method in French, the child's own animal as their character, and the quest village as the story. Two **decodable books** (*Sam Sat* in English and *Le rat et la lune* in French) use only the sounds taught so far.

### DragonBox

- **What it is:** maths games (algebra, numbers, geometry) in which the maths *is* the game mechanic.
- **Why it works:** **intrinsic integration**: the learning content is the core of the play, not a quiz between rounds of play (**Habgood and Ainsworth, 2011**, *Journal of the Learning Sciences*). Children who learned with an intrinsically integrated game learned more and chose to play longer.
- **Evidence:** mixed, but it includes a positive randomised trial with about 1,850 middle-school students.
- **What Kilalao takes:** every Learn activity is something the child *does* with the maths: counting apples into a basket, building numbers from tens and ones, steering a walker with arrows. Answers are given through the activity, not through a multiple-choice quiz screen.

### Coding apps: ScratchJr, Code Karts, Lightbot

- **What they do:** directional sequencing (put arrows in order to move a character) and debugging (find and fix the wrong step).
- **Why they work:** they make an abstract idea (a program is an ordered list of instructions) visible and testable. The child runs the program and sees what happens.
- **What Kilalao takes:** the **Thinking & coding** track (18 skills): patterns, odd one out, sorting, coding a path with arrows (the walker is the child's quest friend), and debugging a program with one wrong arrow. The free-play *Code Factory* toy sits in the same cubby.

### Social-emotional apps: Daniel Tiger, Sesame Street "Breathe, Think, Do"

- **What they do:** help children name feelings, link them to causes, and use a simple calming strategy (slow breathing).
- **Why they work:** naming emotions and understanding their causes is the core of early emotional literacy, and a concrete breathing routine is something a child can use anywhere.
- **What Kilalao takes:** the **Feelings** track (7 skills): find a face that shows a feeling, say how a friend feels in a little scene and why, choose what could help a sad friend, and calm breathing ("smell the flower, blow out the candle", three slow breaths).

### Pok Pok

- **What it is:** a calm, open-ended, wordless digital playroom.
- **Why it works:** no goals, no failure and low stimulation let children explore at their own pace. It is the closest app to Kilalao's free-play toys.
- **What Kilalao takes:** confirmation of the original design: the 17 free-play toys stay wordless and open-ended, and the whole app keeps a calm palette and slow motion.

## What Kilalao already had before this update

- **Adaptive mastery** with Bayesian Knowledge Tracing, placement by age and spaced review.
- **Worked-example fading:** *watch*, then *together*, then *your turn*.
- **A hint ladder:** look again, first step, show me.
- **Process feedback** ("You touched each one once"), never praise of the person.
- **The quest village:** the child chooses whom to help (autonomy), and friends move in (relatedness).
- **Offline use, no ads, no data collection,** and three languages (English, French, Malagasy).

## The four pillars in Kilalao

| Pillar | How Kilalao meets it |
|---|---|
| Active | The child does the maths, reading, coding or breathing inside the activity; the hint ladder asks the child to finish every answer. |
| Engaged | No ads, no rewards, no streaks, no hotspots unrelated to the content. Sounds and motion are calm and tied to what the child is doing. |
| Meaningful | Quests help a friend with a real-world need (apples in a basket, sharing fruit). Themes group lessons with related free play. Try-it-together cards link each skill to home life. |
| Socially interactive | Story books end with an open question to talk about. The adult corner shows what the child is working on and suggests an activity to do together. |

## Limits and open questions

- Most of the apps above have little independent evidence. The design choices rest mainly on the general research (the four pillars, intrinsic integration, systematic phonics, worked examples), not on trials of specific apps.
- Kilalao itself has not been tested with children yet. The protocol is in [CHILD-TESTING.md](CHILD-TESTING.md).
- French and Malagasy wording, including the try-it-together cards, needs native-speaker review.
